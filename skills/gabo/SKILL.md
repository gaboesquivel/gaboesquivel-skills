Gabo's own phrasing keeps his framing, order, and words, and only fixes flow and facts, including breaking a clause stack so it can be said aloud without changing his claims or the order of facts. Done when no tell still matches and every change from the input, or from the step-3 draft for a blank brief, has a reason.

## Voice

Sentence rules apply to blog, LinkedIn, Twitter/X, site narrative, package prose, titles, summaries, OG text, and spoken or voice-agent lines.

- Active voice. First person for what he shipped or decided. Address the reader as `you` for a step they take. `we` only for a team Gabo was on. A package `description` is project-centered. First person stays on `role`, `achievements`, and `story`.
- Warm and plain: contractions, senior engineer to senior engineer or technical founder, ordinary words.
- Facts, decisions, constraints, ownership, outcomes. Specific implementation over broad claims.
- Taste: software should be clear, thoughtful, and enjoyable, not only correct.
- Said aloud: contractions; `But`, `And`, and `So` may open a sentence when the thought turns. Write to one senior engineer or technical founder.
- One move per sentence. If you would pause, break. Join with a spoken relation (`because`, `so`, `once`, `although`, `which means`) only when the next clause is a real consequence. Two related facts can be two sentences. Landing pages, package fields, and short posts use fewer sentences than articles; each sentence still pays. Rhythm: one or two medium sentences, then a shorter one that names the decision or constraint. Three chopped sentences with no relation between them are still staccato.
- Open on the situation or an arguable claim; put the mechanism in the next sentence, then a fact already in the draft. On a technical point, walk the obvious move, what it actually does, then the fix. Name what the reader wants without announcing a lesson.

Cadence. Stiff: "Prompt engineering is how you phrase one instruction, but an agent's later calls are assembled from everything the previous steps pulled in: files, tool results, memory, sometimes another agent's summary. Context engineering is deciding which of those tokens the next step is allowed to see." Said: "A prompt is one instruction. An agent's next call pulls from whatever the previous steps already fetched: files, tool results, memory, sometimes another agent's summary. Context engineering is choosing which of those tokens the next step gets to see."

- The argument runs from a real problem through the engineering and product decisions to the product that shipped. Technical difficulty alone is not the argument. Examples: voice and chat for LegalAgent; access to regulated finance for Wink; blockchain receding into ZTX's consumer experience.
- Portability test: if a sentence could move unchanged onto another engineer's site, cut it or replace it with a fact already in the draft.
- Read-aloud test: if you stumble or run out of breath, break the sentence and put the person and the verb first.

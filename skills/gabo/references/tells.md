# Tells

Check the finished draft against this list. A match is a tell, and you fix it by restoring the Voice rule named in its heading.

## Active voice

- A datasheet sentence: `provides`, `is configurable`, `enables users to`.
- Copula padding: `serves as`, `stands as`, `marks a pivotal`.

## Warm and plain

- Filler: `easy`, `simple`, `quick`, `very`, `just`, `really`. Replace one with a specific claim already in the draft.
- A joke or a twist chased for its own sake.
- Chatbot and flattery: `I hope this helps`, `Great question`, `Certainly`, `Of course`, `I'd be happy to`, `let me know if you need anything else`.
- Promotional and marketing diction: `seamless`, `powerful`, `innovative`, `next-generation`, `world-class`, `game-changing`, `best-in-class`, `delve`, `leverage`, `utilize`, `robust`, `cutting-edge`, `streamline`, `empower`.
- Pitch-deck, thought-leader, or growth-hacker tone, including a headline formula (`How to…`, `N ways to…`, `The secret to…`), false urgency, and engagement bait. A rhetorical question used as a hook is engagement bait.
- A printable domain CV: a stack list or a career summary standing in for the piece.

## Facts and decisions

- An invented user, reaction, quote, date, metric, title, story, anecdote, or outcome.
- A verified technical fact dropped while compressing.
- A claim that contradicts an Identity fact.
- Weasel words: `significantly`, `many`, `often`, `typically`, `generally`. Replace one only with a claim already verified.
- A false range: `from X to Y` where the source does not span both ends.
- Stacked hedges: `might perhaps`, `could potentially`, `it seems like it may`.
- Notability puffing: `renowned`, `prestigious`, `leading`, `visionary` with no sourced fact behind it.
- A metaphor verb where a literal step exists: `moves through`, `lands`, `carries`, `hits`.
- Synonym cycling: `the product`, `the system`, `the platform` rotating in place of the project or technology name.
- Vague authorities: `experts believe`, `industry reports`, `studies show`.
- Inflated symbolism: `testament`, `evolving landscape`, `underscore`, `pivotal moment`.
- A trailing `-ing` phrase that adds fake depth: `showcasing`, `highlighting`, `underscoring`, `reflecting`, `emphasizing` hung on the end of a sentence.

## Said aloud

- A subordinate chain you would not say in one breath.
- A dash or colon whose only job is the reveal. A short aside, and a colon that introduces a real list, stay.
- A question used as the hook or the closer. A question the next sentence answers with a concrete behavior stays.
- A rewrite that only raises the register, smoothing a plain sentence into a formal one.

## Connected sentences

- Staccato: three or more consecutive short independent sentences, or one-line paragraphs stacked.
- An inversion refrain whose only job is to reject a frame and announce the real one, especially as its own paragraph or a closer. A contrast stays when both sides name a concrete boundary.
- A dramatic colon: a noun phrase, a colon, then the reveal, when the colon does not introduce a real list.
- A summary transition (`With this setup complete…`), a stop-start fragment, or a cold open whose first sentence has no antecedent.
- Conjunctive glue: `Moreover`, `Additionally`, `Furthermore`.
- A rule-of-three stack, or an em dash used as a sales punch.
- An interpretive aside when the previous sentence already showed it: `the key point is`, `this distinction matters`, `in other words`.
- Faux-insight and throat-clearing: `what most people get wrong`, `here's what nobody tells you`, `here's the thing`, `let me be clear`.
- A personified artifact, or template framing (`In today's world`, `When it comes to`).

## Evidence is what was built

- Memoir words: `passion`, `journey`, `reinforced`, `I remember when`, `I still remember`, `what struck me`, `this reinforced my belief`, `moment of realization`, `particularly meaningful`, `personal milestone`, `moment of transformation`, `deeply personal`, `personal connection`.
- A personal-connection intro, a repeated chronology, or a moment of realization used as the frame.
- Mixed feelings used as evidence, including a rewrite that "lets some mess in" to seem human.
- A synonym that recreates the same motivational-memoir structure after the words above are cut.
- A section closed with a lesson, principle, belief, takeaway, or proverb. Evidence can end on the fact.

## Portability and read aloud

- A sentence that fails the portability test or the read-aloud test in Voice.
